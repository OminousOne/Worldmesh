## Your game is a plugin

This is the whole of tag, the example game, apart from walking: a visible component, a command, two
actions and an event. Nothing in it knows about servers. `To::Entity(target)` reaches the target
wherever it's simulated, and the target judges the tag by where both players stood on that tick.
[Build it step by step](/Worldmesh/getting-started/first-game/).

## What Worldmesh handles

<div class="wm-feature-grid">
<div>

### Split by load, not by map

Server Entity Groups simulate entities wherever they stand. Spatial Layer Cells own areas and
answer "what's near me?". Scale either one while the world runs.

</div>
<div>

### Handoffs mid-stride

When groups are added or removed, entities move between them while they keep moving. The new group
picks up on the next tick: no pause, no lost input, no action applied twice.

</div>
<div>

### Actions land anywhere

An arrow aimed at an entity on another machine, even one mid-handoff, arrives on the next tick and
is never applied twice. The target's side judges it with positions it can trust.

</div>
<div>

### Smooth in the browser

`@worldmesh/client` interpolates everyone else and predicts your own entity bit for bit with your
movement rules, adapting to the connection.

</div>
<div>

### See inside

`@worldmesh/debug` shows who simulates what and whose area is whose, scales the world, adds fake
lag per server, and draws every message on the bus.

</div>
<div>

### Test without servers

`TestWorld` runs a whole world in one process, tick by tick, with real groups and cells passing
real messages.

</div>
</div>
